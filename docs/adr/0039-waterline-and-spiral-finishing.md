# ADR-0039: Waterline contours from a map of tip heights, spirals along rows and columns

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Deciders:** Federico Vezzoli
- **Related:** PRD-0005 (S4, S10), ADR-0030, ADR-0032, ADR-0033, ADR-0035; issue #130

## Context

3D finishing only cut parallel passes (ADR-0032). Their step-over is measured flat, so on a wall running along them the passes are far apart in Z and leave tall scallops. PRD-0005 S10 asks for waterline (constant Z) and spiral finishing. Both have to reuse ADR-0030's offset heightmap and tool dropping, report progress, be cancellable, and never cut into the model.

## Decision

- **Strategy in the document:** `ReliefFinishParams.strategy` is `{ type: "parallel", angle, direction } | { type: "waterline", stepDown } | { type: "spiral", start: "outside" | "centre" }`. Schema v12 moves existing operations' angle and direction into a parallel strategy. `stepOver` (parallel passes and spiral laps) and `stockToLeave` stay shared; waterline ignores the step-over, and the UI hides it. Picking waterline starts at a 0.5 mm step-down, a spiral at the outline.
- **Grid:** waterline and spiral rasterise along the stock's axes (angle 0), so they share cached heightmaps with each other and with parallel passes at 0°. All three start from the same setup (`finishSetupSteps`): grid, offset heights, floor, and the rest stock for rest machining.
- **Waterline:** the tool is dropped once at every cell into a map of tip heights (the lowest its tip may go there). Levels go from the stock top down to the floor in equal steps no deeper than the step-down, like roughing's (ADR-0033). At each level, the contours where the map crosses the level are traced between cell centres with marching squares (interpolated along each side of a square, not along cell edges, so the tool doesn't zig-zag; a saddle's centre value decides whether its high corners join). Contours keep the model on the tool's right (climb milling with a clockwise spindle), are open where they meet the grid's edge (the tool's centre stays inside the model's outline), and keep only the points where the direction changes. A contour point between two cells more than half a cell apart in height is checked by dropping the tool there, widened by 1 µm; if it would reach above the level, it moves back towards the cell below the level by halving, so it keeps a real clearance rather than touching the model (and rounding into it in `float32` coordinates). On those steep sides the straight move to the next point is checked too, at its middle, with the tool as it is: around a sharp corner a move between two clear points sags towards the model by up to a cell's length squared over four times the tool's radius, under the 1 µm clearance at full resolution but not on coarsened grids. Where the middle isn't clear, a point is added there, moved away from the model until it is, and both halves are checked again, up to six times. Levels are cut top down, each from the nearest contour on; a closed contour starts at its point nearest the tool. The tool feeds straight to the next contour when it is within two tool diameters and every cell-spaced point of the move clears the model; otherwise it retracts and plunges in. Only the 16 × 16 blocks of squares whose values straddle a level are scanned.
- **Spiral:** a rectangular spiral over the cell centres inside the outline, from the bottom-left corner inwards and counter-clockwise, its laps as many columns and rows apart as fit in the step-over. Each lap steps in along the next lap's first row; once no lap fits, the last one is closed and a run along its middle row or column covers what is left inside when it is wider than a step. A centre start runs the same spiral backwards. Every side lies on a row or a column of cell centres, so the tool is dropped with the sliding maximum of ADR-0032 along rows, and along columns on a transposed copy of the heights. Samples are merged with `simplifyHeights`. Where the tool rises or drops by more than a cell's size between two samples, it goes over at the higher of the two instead of in a straight line, since the model may rise above a straight move there (a wall a flat end mill drops off anywhere between the samples).
- **Rest machining:** waterline contours and spiral runs only cut where the tool meets more than `REST_MATERIAL` of the stock left (ADR-0035), gaps narrower than the tool joined, retracting across the others. Contours are checked every cell along them, not only at the points left after merging straight stretches, so stock left in the middle of a long straight contour is found.
- **Progress:** the setup takes the first 40% as before. Waterline's tip map takes half of the rest, the levels the other half; a spiral reports after each run.

## Consequences

- On an Apple M1 Pro in Node, finishing a synthetic 300 × 200 mm relief (120,000 triangles) with a 3 mm ball end mill at a 10% step-over takes about 2.5 s with parallel passes, 2.6 s with a spiral and 9.4 s with waterline at a 0.5 mm step-down. Waterline's time is almost all the tip map, which drops the tool at every cell rather than every few rows.
- Waterline leaves flat areas between contours uncut, as waterline strategies do. Combining waterline on steep faces with parallel passes on shallow ones (by slope) could come later.
- In a spiral's corners a point can be up to `step / √2` from the nearest lap, as with offset rings; along the sides it is at most half a step.
- A spiral on steep faces goes over in small steps of up to a cell's size, a slightly coarser finish there than a straight move would leave; waterline is the strategy for steep faces.
- Tools are still dropped onto cell centres (ADR-0030), so a cutter's rim can reach up to a cell's worth of a slope between them, as with parallel passes. Testing against the simulation also showed that parallel passes cut straight between samples across a wall a flat end mill drops off, nicking the wall's top edge; this ADR doesn't change parallel passes, and a follow-up can give them the spiral's rule.
